## Pattern

In community or info product pricing, raising prices can increase signup velocity when scarcity and perceived value are aligned. The mechanism: a hard member cap creates genuine scarcity, higher price signals higher value, and the combination triggers urgency that lower prices don't.

This inverts the normal price-demand relationship — but only under specific conditions.

## Evidence

Saraev's MMWM community:
- 400-member hard cap
- Launched at $28/month
- Raised to $128/month (4.6x increase)
- Filled faster at the higher price (~10 days)

## Apply When

- Pricing community products where demand exceeds capacity
- Only when there's a genuine capacity constraint (member cap, cohort size, service capacity)
- After demonstrating demand at a lower price point — you need proof of demand before testing this

## Boundaries

- Without the cap, raising prices just reduces volume — the cap creates the scarcity signal
- Requires demonstrated demand first — you can't start at the high price without proof
- Doesn't apply to self-serve digital products with no capacity constraint
- Risk: perceived as manipulative if the cap feels artificial
