## Pattern

Buyers pay to eliminate recurring friction, not to acquire impressive capabilities. A workflow that saves 2 hours every weekday is worth 50× more than one that saves 2 hours once.

The 1% of solutions that generate consistent commercial demand share: high-frequency trigger, low judgment requirement, directly measurable ROI.

## Evidence

After building 500 AI workflows, only 5 (~1%) generate consistent commercial demand. All five are "boring":
1. AI receptionist
2. Review request bot
3. Support/FAQ agent
4. Newsletter ghostwriter
5. Personalized outreach writer

Revenue documented: $23,000 total across 4 agents. Individual sales: $1,200–$6,000 per workflow.

## Decision Rule

When prioritizing which automation workflows to build for clients or as productized services: lead with pain frequency, not technical sophistication. The "boring" category is the profitable category.

## Boundaries

- n=1 practitioner (Nate Herk) — the 5 categories are specific but from a single source
- Fails in markets where novelty is the purchase driver (consumer entertainment, luxury)
- Fails when "boring" means "already fully automated" — value requires the pain to currently be manual
