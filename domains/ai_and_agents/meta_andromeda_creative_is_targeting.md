## Pattern

Meta's Andromeda retrieval algorithm (running on NVIDIA GH200 infrastructure) assigns an Entity ID based on each ad's visual/audio fingerprint. Upload 20 similar ads and they all share one Entity ID — meaning one auction ticket. Conceptual diversity earns multiple tickets, each competing independently in the auction.

Andromeda is 100x faster at matching people to ads and handles 10,000x more variants in parallel than its predecessor. This fundamentally inverts the old Meta ads discipline: tight ad sets with narrow targeting are now counterproductive. The algorithm overrides detailed targeting anyway — it's advisory, not binding.

## Optimal Structure

- **1 campaign**, **1-2 broad ad sets**, **10-20 conceptually distinct creatives**
- Conceptual diversity > volume of similar variants
- CPMr (cost per 1,000 reach) is the fatigue early-warning metric
- Detailed targeting is advisory — Meta overrides it

## Apply When

- Structuring any TNT Growth client Meta account
- Writing creative briefs — shift from "more variations" to "more concepts"
- Evaluating client account structures that still use granular ad set segmentation
- Setting production cadence — need a pipeline for conceptually distinct creative, not minor iterations

## Boundaries

- Entity ID fingerprinting means minor visual tweaks (color changes, text swaps) don't earn new auction tickets
- CPMr monitoring needed to catch creative fatigue before CPA spikes

## What Changed

Old discipline of tight, segmented ad sets is now counterproductive. Creative strategy IS the targeting strategy. Volume wins, restraint loses — but only conceptual volume, not variant volume.
